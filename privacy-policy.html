Summary

In short: Runtide has no accounts and no servers of ours. Your health data is processed on your iPhone, and we receive it only if you choose to send us a report about a run. It keeps your settings and plan setups in your own iCloud. If you turn on the optional weather lookup, an approximate location goes to a weather service. If you connect Strava, the runs you upload go to your Strava account. We only receive what you choose to send us.

1. What Runtide reads

With your permission, Runtide reads from Apple Health:

  • your workouts and their routes, including other activities such as cycling
  • what was recorded during them: heart rate, distance, pace, steps, cadence, running power, stride length, vertical oscillation, ground contact time, energy burned, and cycling speed, cadence and power
  • sleep, resting heart rate, heart rate variability, heart rate recovery, breathing rate and wrist temperature
  • VO₂ max and workout effort

It uses them to analyse your runs, track your training load and recovery, and build training plans from your own history.

This is health information, which privacy law treats as sensitive. Runtide processes it on your iPhone, and we receive it only if you choose to send us a report about a run (section 7).

You choose what Runtide can read, and you can change it at any time in the Settings app: Health > Data Access & Devices > Runtide.

Runs read in the background

Once you've given access, Apple Health wakes Runtide when a workout is added, so a run you've just finished is in your plan, your widgets and your Apple Watch's next days without opening the app. Runtide reads only the newest workouts then, on your iPhone, and never while it's locked (Apple Health doesn't allow it). Nothing is sent anywhere, and your plan doesn't change: anything a run would change still waits for you to choose it in the app.

2. Where it lives

Your health data stays on your iPhone, unless you choose to send a report about a run (section 7). What Runtide works out from your workouts (your training history, your plans and your settings) is stored in the app's own storage on the phone, protected by your passcode.

If you add a Runtide widget, it shows your planned session on your Home Screen or Lock Screen, where anyone holding the phone can see it. To make that work, Runtide keeps a small file with your planned sessions' days, names, lengths and kinds that stays readable while the phone is locked, once you've unlocked it after a restart. It stays on your iPhone, isn't in device backups, and goes when you delete Runtide's data.

Device backups (iCloud Backup or a backup to a computer) are separate from Runtide's own iCloud sync below. Your training history, plans, availability, off-plan answers, session feedback, weather results and crash reports are left out of device backups; Runtide reads your runs from Health again on a new phone, and each plan's setup reaches it through iCloud as described below. Your settings, heart-rate numbers and zones included, are part of device backups like other app data.

3. What's in your iCloud

If you use iCloud, Runtide keeps two things in your own iCloud account, so they reach your other iPhones and a new one:

  • your settings: appearance, distance unit, the Apple Watch and weather choices, and whether you finished the first-run screens
  • each plan's setup: the race, its date and target time, the plan's dates and your training preferences (runs a week, long-run day, build pace and the like)

We can't see them. They're stored with Apple's iCloud key-value storage under your Apple Account, and Runtide has no server that reads them.

Never synced through iCloud: Health data, your training history, heart-rate settings and zones, threshold and training paces, availability (including illness and injury), session feedback, RPE and heart-rate corrections. Runtide keeps these on your iPhone; your heart-rate settings and zones are in device backups with your other settings (above).

Because the weather choice syncs, turning the weather lookup on or off on one iPhone does so on your others.

4. What leaves your iPhone

Apart from your own iCloud (above), anything you choose to send (sections 5 and 7) and the runs you upload to Strava (below), only the weather lookup. It is off until you turn it on, when you first open Runtide or later in Settings > "Look Up Weather". Nothing is sent while it is off.

With it on, Runtide asks Open-Meteo (open-meteo.com), a weather service, for the temperature and conditions of an outdoor run saved without weather, the wind and "feels like" temperature of outdoor runs, and the forecast for your planned sessions. To do that it sends:

  • where a run started, rounded to about 1 km (for the forecast, where your most recent outdoor run started)
  • the date of the run or forecast

It sends no name, account, device identifier or Health values. As with any internet request, Open-Meteo can see your device's IP address. That is how the request reaches it, and we don't receive it.

Strava

Nothing goes to Strava unless you connect it in Runtide > Settings > Strava, which signs you in on Strava's own page. Runtide then uploads a workout when you tap Upload to Strava on its screen, and, if you turn on "Upload New Runs", each run that ends after you turned it on, once Runtide has read it. For each workout it uploads, Runtide sends its route, times, distance, heart rate, cadence, power and laps, as a file Strava turns into an activity on your account. Before each upload it asks Strava for the start and length of your activities that day, private ones included, so a workout already on Strava isn't uploaded twice; it keeps nothing from that list but the matching activity's link. This goes from your iPhone straight to Strava; we don't receive it. Strava's own privacy policy covers it from then on. The access Strava gives Runtide is kept in this iPhone's Keychain, never in iCloud or backups. Disconnecting in Settings, or Delete Runtide Data, removes Runtide's access on Strava; what was uploaded stays on Strava until you delete it there.

Open-Meteo, Strava, Apple and, if you email us, our email provider (Google) handle information under their own terms, and may do so outside your country.

5. What we receive

We have no access to the information on your iPhone or in your iCloud. We receive personal information only when:

  • You contact us. If you email us or use Send Feedback, we receive your email address and whatever you write or attach. We use it to answer you and to improve Runtide.
  • You report a problem with a run. If you send us a report from Report a Problem with This Run (section 7), we receive your email address, what you write and the file, which holds health and fitness information about that one run. We use it only to fix that problem.
  • You send us diagnostics. If you share a file from Share Diagnostics with us (section 7), we receive its crash and performance reports.
  • You agreed to share with app developers. If you turned on sharing with app developers in your iPhone's settings, Apple gives us crash reports and summarised usage figures for Runtide. You can turn this off in Settings > Privacy & Security > Analytics & Improvements.

If you take part in a TestFlight beta, Apple also gives us the details TestFlight collects, such as your tester name and email address and any feedback you send through it.

6. Apple Watch, notifications and writing to Health

If you turn on "Upcoming Workouts on Apple Watch" in Runtide's Settings, Runtide puts your planned runs for the next 7 days in the Workout app on your watch, through Apple's WorkoutKit. This happens between your own devices.

If you allow notifications, Runtide sends one local notification after a run: that your race result is ready to look at, that the run wasn't what the plan had, or that the run is ready to review. They're made on your iPhone; there's no push service and nothing about them leaves the phone. They never show anything about the run itself (no pace, heart rate or distance), so nothing about your health appears on the Lock Screen. If you allow notifications, Runtide also reminds you of a planned session on its morning, at a time you choose: the session's name, distance and target pace from your plan and, with the weather lookup on, the forecast already fetched for where you run. These reminders are scheduled on your iPhone and nothing about them leaves it. Turn them off in Runtide > Settings > Notifications or in the Settings app > Notifications > Runtide.

Runtide saves to Health only when you ask it to merge two workouts into one. The merged workout keeps their route, heart rate, distance and other samples.

7. Backup files, run reports and diagnostics

Settings > Backup & iCloud > Back Up My Data makes one file, only when you ask, and saves it where you choose in the Files picker. It holds your settings including heart-rate zones, your plans with the run summaries your current plans were built from, availability including illness and injury, and session feedback. It doesn't hold your Health data or the training history Runtide reads from it. Treat the file like any personal file: anyone who has it can read it. Restore from Backup replaces what's on your iPhone with it. If you use iCloud, the restored plan setups then sync to your other iPhones; plans in iCloud that aren't in the file aren't deleted from your other iPhones.

If a run looks wrong, you can choose Report a Problem with This Run on its screen. Runtide makes a file about that one run and opens an email to us, or the share sheet if you don't use Mail, and nothing is sent until you tap Send. The file holds how Runtide read the run and the session it matched; the run's heart rate, pace, cadence, power and height changes, second by second; its laps, steps and pauses; the day you ran it and your UTC offset; your heart rate zones; the app that recorded it and your device's model; and the app's version. It leaves out your route and where you ran, your name and your device's name, the time of day, the weather, your sleep and your other health readings. Your email address goes with it. The report arrives in our inbox, is used only to fix that problem and is deleted once it's fixed.

Otherwise, the App Store version of Runtide can't export your workouts to files. If you choose Share Diagnostics in Settings > About, Runtide makes a file of its crash and performance reports (no Health data) and you decide where to send it.

8. What Runtide does not do

  • No accounts and no servers of ours.
  • No analytics of its own, advertising or tracking.
  • No third-party code (SDKs) inside the app.
  • No selling of your data, and no sharing of it for advertising.

We may transfer what we hold (section 5) as part of a merger, sale of company assets, financing or acquisition, and we may disclose it where the law requires. The only health data of yours we can hold is a run report you sent us (section 7).

Runtide's plans and analysis are worked out automatically on your iPhone. They are training suggestions and have no legal or similarly significant effect on you.

9. Our legal bases

If you are in the EU, EEA or UK, these are the legal bases for processing your personal information:

  • Consent, including explicit consent for health data: Runtide reading Apple Health once you allow it, the weather lookup, uploads to Strava once you connect it, and a report about a run, which you choose to send each time. Change the permissions, turn the lookup off or disconnect Strava at any time. Withdrawing consent doesn't affect what happened before it.
  • Legitimate interests: answering your messages, and fixing and improving Runtide from reports you or Apple send us.
  • Legal obligations: where the law requires us to keep or disclose information.
  • Vital interests: in the rare case it's needed to protect someone's life.

If you are in Canada, we rely on your express or implied consent, or on an exception the law allows.

10. How long information is kept

What's on your iPhone stays until you delete it (section 11). Crash and performance reports on the phone are limited to the 10 newest of the last 30 days.

We keep your messages and anything sent with them only as long as needed to deal with them and improve Runtide, unless the law requires longer. Then we delete or anonymise them. A report about a run is deleted once its problem is fixed.

11. Deleting your data

In Runtide, Settings > Delete Runtide Data deletes everything it stored on your iPhone (plans, training history, feedback, settings, weather results and crash reports), removes its upcoming workouts from your Apple Watch and its notifications, and removes its access to your Strava account (runs already uploaded stay on Strava until you delete them there); Apple Health stops waking it until you set it up again. It also removes your settings and plan setups from iCloud, and those plans from your other iPhones. A backup file you saved stays where you saved it; delete it there.

Deleting Runtide from your iPhone also removes everything it stored on the phone, but not what it keeps in your iCloud: your settings and plan setups stay there and come back if you install Runtide again. To remove them from iCloud, use Delete Runtide Data (before deleting the app, or after installing it again). Turning iCloud off for Runtide (Settings > [your name] > iCloud) stops the sync but doesn't delete what's already in iCloud.

Your Health data stays in Health, where you manage it yourself; Runtide only saved to it the workouts you merged.

To have us delete what you've sent us, email us (section 17).

12. Your privacy rights

Depending on where you live (including the EEA, UK, Switzerland and Canada), you may have the right to access and get a copy of your personal information, to correct or erase it, to restrict or object to its processing, to data portability, and to withdraw consent. Because we don't receive what's on your iPhone, you can do most of this yourself in Runtide and the Health app. For what we do hold, email us (section 17) and we'll respond in line with the law.

You also have the right to complain to a data protection authority:

  • In Portugal, the Comissão Nacional de Protecção de Dados (cnpd.pt).
  • Elsewhere in the EEA, your national authority.
  • In the UK, the Information Commissioner's Office (ico.org.uk/make-a-complaint). You can complain to us first: we'll acknowledge it within 30 days, investigate without undue delay and explain the outcome.
  • In Switzerland, the Federal Data Protection and Information Commissioner.

13. United States residents

Some US states give you the right to know what personal information a business holds about you, to access, correct and delete it, to get a copy, and to opt out of its sale, targeted advertising and certain profiling. You can use these rights by emailing us (section 17), or through someone you've authorised. If we refuse a request, you can appeal by replying to us. We won't treat you differently for using them.

We do not sell personal information or share it for targeted advertising, and we have not done so in the past 12 months. We don't receive precise location: it stays on your iPhone, unless you connect Strava, when the routes of the runs you upload go to your Strava account. Health information stays there too, unless you choose to send us a report about a run. The categories we may receive are in section 5: identifiers (your email address), the health information in a run report, and what you send us. The approximate location in section 4 goes to Open-Meteo for the weather you asked for, and only when the lookup is on.

Runtide doesn't respond to Do-Not-Track signals, because it doesn't track you and no standard for them has been finalised.

14. Security

Runtide's files on your iPhone use iOS file protection, tied to your passcode, requests to Open-Meteo and Strava use HTTPS, and Runtide's access to Strava is kept in the iPhone's Keychain. We take reasonable technical and organisational steps to protect what we hold. No transmission or storage can be guaranteed to be 100% secure, so keep your iPhone's passcode and any backup file safe.
