Runtide Support

Running plans built from your own runs.

I don't see my runs

Runtide reads your runs from Apple Health. In the Settings app, go to Health > Data Access & Devices > Runtide and turn on Workouts, Heart Rate and Workout Routes. Runs recorded with Garmin, Strava or other apps appear only if those apps save them to Health. The first read of a long history can take a few minutes: keep your iPhone unlocked meanwhile, because Health can't be read while it's locked.

Why does Runtide need each Health permission?

  • Workouts and workout routes: to list your runs, map them and look up their weather.
  • Heart rate: for heart-rate zones, training load and easy-run checks.
  • Pace, cadence and running power: for splits, intervals and how each run went.
  • Sleep, resting heart rate, heart rate variability, breathing rate and wrist temperature: to show how recovered you were before a run.
  • VO₂ max and workout effort: to follow your fitness and estimate the load of runs without heart rate.

Runtide saves to Health only when you ask it to merge two workouts into one.

Heart-rate zones

Zones are set as a percentage of your maximum heart rate by default. In Runtide's Settings you can switch to a percentage of your threshold heart rate or set your own zones. If your maximum heart rate is wrong, your training load and easy-run checks will be off too, so it's worth setting it from a recent hard effort.

How do plans adapt?

Plans are built from your recent running. When you miss a key session, run more or less than planned, take a break, get ill, or run something different from the planned session, the Plan tab tells you and offers to change the coming week. You see the week before and after the change, and you can keep the plan as it is. When Runtide gets a better way of building plans, you'll see "A better plan is available"; your plan changes only if you tap it.

Apple Watch

Turn on Upcoming Workouts in Runtide's Settings (Apple Watch section) to put the next 7 days of your plan in the Workout app on your watch. They update when the plan changes, and turning it off removes them.

Weather

With "Look Up Weather" on, a finished run shows its conditions under How it felt: the weather your watch saved, or Open-Meteo's for where and when it started, with the wind and "feels like" temperature added. A planned session from today to a week ahead shows the forecast for its day and the hours you usually run, for where your last outdoor run started. Runtide sends Open-Meteo only where runs started, rounded to about 1 km, and when. The lookup is off until you turn it on, when you first open Runtide, in Settings > Weather > "Look Up Weather", or from a run's Conditions; turn it off in Settings to stop it.

Your usual hours come from when you started runs in the last 12 weeks (up to two windows, such as 7–9 and 18–19). With fewer than five runs the forecast shows 7:00, 12:00 and 18:00.

Units

Runtide shows distances and paces in kilometres or miles. It follows your iPhone's region until you pick one in Settings > Display > Distance. Plans are still built in kilometres, so some distances won't be round numbers in miles.

Moving to a new iPhone

If you use iCloud, your settings and each plan's setup come back by themselves on the new iPhone. Runtide then builds those plans again from your runs once Apple Health has them there. Health data moves with Apple's own Health sync in iCloud or an iPhone backup, and Runtide reads it again.

For everything else (heart-rate zones, session feedback, availability and your plans exactly as they were), use Settings > Back Up My Data on the old iPhone, save the file somewhere the new one can reach, then Settings > Restore from Backup on the new one. A restore replaces what's on that iPhone only: plans on your other iPhones stay, and those that don't overlap the restored ones appear on it through iCloud.

Deleting your data

In Runtide, go to Settings > Delete Runtide Data. It deletes your plans, the training history read from Health, feedback, settings and weather results, removes upcoming workouts from your Apple Watch, and removes your settings and plan setups from iCloud (and those plans from your other iPhones). Your workouts in Apple Health aren't changed. Deleting the app alone doesn't remove what's in iCloud, and turning iCloud off for Runtide (Settings > [your name] > iCloud) only stops the sync; use Delete Runtide Data to remove your settings and plan setups from iCloud. See also the privacy policy.

Medical note

Runtide isn't medical advice. If you feel unwell, stop running and see a doctor.
